**Yes. A psychic reading can reveal whether someone is thinking about you, the feelings connected to those thoughts, and what they mean for your relationship. To look at thoughts and feelings, I use clairvoyance and clairaudience, with Tarot to clarify what comes through.**

Perhaps you have not heard from someone in a while, but you cannot stop thinking about them. Or you have met someone new, and you are wondering whether you made the same impression on them that they made on you. You replay the conversation, remember the look on their face, and then try to decide whether you imagined the whole thing.

Wouldn't it be nice if people simply told you what they were thinking? Of course, sometimes they do—and then you have to figure out what they meant by that, too.

I have been reading professionally since 1999. Questions about love and relationships are central to my work, and understanding what is going on with the other person is an important part of that. But once you know someone is thinking about you, there is usually another question waiting right behind it: What are they thinking, and why?

## How can I tell if someone is thinking about you?

I use clairvoyance and clairaudience to look at thoughts and feelings, and Tarot to clarify what comes through. You can learn more about [my approach to psychic readings](/psychic-reading) and how I tune into the people involved in your question.

I also experience telepathy, but for me it happens spontaneously, at random. It is not something I switch on at will.

When you ask about someone, the subject is more personal than whether your name happens to cross their mind. You want to understand your place in their life. Are they remembering time you spent together? Thinking about something you said? Considering reaching out? Or trying to make sense of their own feelings?

Those questions give the reading somewhere useful to go. A yes can be reassuring, but the substance of the thought matters. There is a difference between remembering a pleasant afternoon and thinking seriously about seeing you again. Both concern you; they do not tell the same story.

## Does thinking about you mean someone has feelings for you?

It can, but the thought needs context. People think about one another for many reasons: attraction, affection, curiosity, regret, an unresolved disagreement, or a memory that still matters to them.

Imagine that you recently had a wonderful first date. The other person could be thinking about your conversation and wondering whether you would like to go out again. Now imagine someone you argued with last week. They could also be thinking about you, but trying to decide how to approach the disagreement. You would want to understand those situations differently.

This is where your question can become more specific. Instead of stopping at “Am I on their mind?” you can ask about the feelings around those thoughts and the direction of the connection.

It is perfectly understandable to want confirmation that you matter to someone. Just leave room for the answer to have its own meaning. If you are hoping for romance, a thought about you can feel like the first piece of a love story. We still need to understand what kind of interest is there.

## Why would someone think about you but stay silent?

Someone can think about you while hesitating to contact you. They might be uncertain about your response, embarrassed about a previous exchange, or unsure what they want to say. They might also enjoy thinking about you without having decided to take the connection further.

Silence leaves a great deal of room for imagination. Suppose you sent a warm message and received no answer. You might decide they are uninterested—or decide their feelings are so powerful that they cannot find the words. Those are very different explanations for the same unanswered message.

A reading can help you explore what is behind that silence. The aim is to understand the person and the situation, rather than fill in every blank with the explanation you most want to hear.

Then, as things unfold, notice how they communicate with you. When they do reach out, what are they saying? Are they making room for a conversation? Do their actions give the connection somewhere to go? The information from your reading and the interaction between you belong together.

## If your ex is thinking about you, does that mean they will come back?

Thinking about you and deciding to return are different things. An ex can remember you, miss you, or think about the breakup while still working through what they want to do next.

If reconciliation is your real concern, say so. That lets us look beyond whether you are on their mind and into the possibility of renewed contact or a reunion. I explore that question in [Can a Psychic Tell If Your Ex Will Come Back?](/can-a-psychic-tell-if-your-ex-will-come-back).

Consider a hypothetical ex who thinks fondly about the good times but remains unwilling to address the issue that ended the relationship. Now consider an ex who is thinking about how to apologize and ask for another chance. Both are thinking about you. What you need to understand is where those thoughts are leading.

Your wishes matter here, too. If a conversation becomes possible, what would you want to say? What would you need to understand before trying again? A reading can help you prepare for that conversation as well as explore whether it is coming.

## What should you ask in a reading about someone's thoughts?

Start with the question that brought you to the reading, then be clear about what you hope to understand. If you want to know whether someone is thinking about you, useful follow-up questions include:

- What are they thinking about in relation to me?
- What feelings are connected to those thoughts?
- Are they considering contacting me?
- What is holding back communication between us?
- Where is this connection heading?

You do not need to turn up with a perfectly prepared interview. Tell me what is concerning you. If the question behind “Are they thinking about me?” is really “Do they want to see me again?” we can address that directly.

Sometimes the answer brings reassurance. Sometimes it helps you understand a situation differently. My intention is to tell you plainly what I read, with care, so you have something meaningful to work with.
